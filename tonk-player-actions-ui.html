Tonk — Player Actions UI

Three distinct interaction models for the Tonk two-phase turn: discard first → then draw, plus a turn-start TONK declaration. Every control is rendered/enabled strictly from the server's validActions per phase — the client never validates Tonk rules itself. Aesthetic intentionally matches the live Big2 felt-table board (same tokens, fonts, card rendering).

Rules driving this UI (LLD 65): A turn is two mandatory phases by the same player. Discard phase — discard one or more same-rank cards, OR (only when the TONK gate is open, i.e. every player has had ≥1 turn) declare TONK instead. Draw phase — draw exactly one card from the stock or the single drawable discard (offered only when the snapshot is non-null; you can never draw back your own just-discarded card). Turn hands off only after the draw. Mixed-rank discard selection is visually rejected, but the server is the authority — rejection produces an inline error and the selection is preserved.
How to read these mockups. Each direction below shows the same sequence of turn states so you can compare them directly:
  • A — Discard phase, idle: your turn just started, nothing selected yet (TONK gate still closed).
  • B — Discard phase, same-rank selected: three Queens picked; other ranks dimmed; Discard armed with a count.
  • C — Mixed-rank rejected: a non-matching card got picked → inline error, selection preserved (server-authoritative).
  • D — Discard phase, TONK gate OPEN: callTonk is now in validActions; the TONK control appears.
  • E — Draw phase: discard committed; now choose stock or the drawable discard (here a 4♦ snapshot).
  • F — Draw phase, stock-only: drawableDiscard === null (trick-1 first player) → only stock is offered.
  • G — Not your turn: all controls disabled.

Direction A — Phase Stepper footer panel, drop-in slot

Keeps the exact footer-bar slot Big2 already uses (game-board__actions). A compact “1 Discard → 2 Draw” stepper at the top of the panel shows where you are in the turn; the panel morphs its buttons per turnPhase. Lowest layout risk — reuses the existing ActionPanel.vue shell and grid. Draw sources are buttons in the footer (the stock count and a thumbnail of the drawable card live on the button).

Direction B — Contextual In-Place Draw draw from where the cards are

Discard phase is identical to A (footer buttons + hand selection). The difference is the draw phase: instead of footer buttons, the stock pile and the drawable-discard card on the table light up as the two clickable targets (gold ring + pulse), and the footer collapses to a thin prompt. You draw from the literal spot the card lives — spatially intuitive, and it keeps your eyes on the table. Costs a bit more layout wiring (the table piles must be interactive).

Direction C — Guided Turn Banner narrated prompt above the hand

A prominent banner sits above the hand and narrates the turn step-by-step, with a two-segment progress bar and the controls inline. Most hand-holding — best for players new to Tonk's unusual discard-first order. The banner explicitly states the rule for the current phase ("discard one or more of the same rank"). TONK is a quiet text link (so it's never the accidental primary action). Heaviest footprint and most text, but the clearest teaching surface.

Side-by-side trade-offs

A — Phase Stepper

Morphing footer panel + “1 → 2” stepper. Reuses the Big2 ActionPanel slot and grid almost verbatim.

Best for: shipping fast with minimal layout risk and a consistent, compact control bar. The safe default.

B — Contextual Draw

Draw happens by tapping the glowing piles on the table; footer is a thin prompt during the draw phase.

Best for: the most spatially intuitive “draw from where the card is” feel. Needs interactive table piles (more wiring).

C — Guided Banner

Narrated banner above the hand teaches the discard-first order explicitly; TONK is a quiet link.

Best for: onboarding players to Tonk's unusual turn order. Largest footprint, most copy to maintain.
