Game Over — Final Cards & Reveal Timer

Two related problems from issue #65: (1) the post-game reveal pause is a forced 4s and one user wanted longer; (2) the results screen never shows the final played cards (and on mobile the cards may not even be visible during the 4s reveal). Below: three options for the reveal pause behaviour, the new "Final Play" row on the results screen, and the mobile variants. The shipped reveal mechanism (SHOW_FINAL_PLAY) stays — we only change how long it lingers and where the cards stay reviewable.

Part 1 · The reveal pause (over the board, desktop)

State machine is unchanged: IN_PROGRESS → SHOW_FINAL_PLAY → COMPLETED. The question is what SHOW_FINAL_PLAY does. The current build dims the whole board, which is why cards "disappear" behind the scrim. All three options replace the literal 4000 with a named constant.

Default behaves like Option A (auto-advance after REVEAL_AUTO_ADVANCE_MS), but a "Stay & review" button cancels the timer (shown here in its paused state — bar greys and freezes). After pausing, only "Continue to Results" remains. Best of both: hands-off players are not stuck, and studiers get unlimited time. Most code of the three.

Part 2 · Final play row on the results screen (desktop)

A compact "Final Play" block is inserted on GameOverView between the winner heading and the scores table. It renders the winning cards (lastPlay.cards) with the hand-type label and who played them — so the cards stay reviewable after the board phase ends. This directly answers "game over screen does not display final cards played," and means even if the reveal phase is skipped (or cards weren't seen on mobile), the cards are still right there.

Recommendation

Part 2 (Final Play row on results) ships regardless of which Part 1 option is chosen — it is the load-bearing fix for both the literal complaint and the mobile case. For Part 1, the recommendation is Option B (no timer, wait for Continue): it fully removes timer pressure, is the least code, and the results-screen cards remove any risk of "losing" the final play. If a hands-off auto-advance is still wanted (e.g. so an idle game eventually frees the screen), Option C is the fallback. All options switch the dim scrim to the spotlight scrim so cards stay visible during the reveal.
